8 - Collective Distribution

The Cogitarium is the part of Yuuko Protocol where devices near each other pass a signed stream to each other, so one source doesn't have to send it to everyone. It was made specifically for the SCCN (Streaming Gaussian Splatting for High-Density AR), its Collective Distribution part, but it only moves signed bytes, so it works for anything else too. It is also built around a streetpass model: devices that pass each other find each other (Nearby), see what streams are around before accepting any (Offers), and swap small cards (MyCard).

Devices in the same chunk (an area) form a mesh. The source signs every piece of the stream and sends it to its best few neighbours. Each device that gets a piece checks the signature, uses it, and passes it to its own best few. With 1000 devices in a room the source only talks to 10 and the rest pass it on, and a device never gets the same piece twice because every piece has a number and repeats are dropped.

The five sizes: Cogitarium.Full, ThreeQuarters, Half, Quarter, Sixteenth (the paper's 1, 3/4, 1/2, 1/4 and 1/16). Settings: InitialReceivers (how many the source sends to, default 10), Fanout (how many each device passes it to, default 3), VotesToDistrust (default 3), MaxSegmentBytes (512 KB, cut bigger things up).

Example: a source and a viewer

//On both
Cogitarium.Start();
Cogitarium.JoinChunk("district-12");

//On the source: give the others this
var Mine = Cogitarium.MyFingerprint;
var Stream = Cogitarium.StartStream("district-12", "Cafe scene");
await Stream.Send(FrameBytes, Cogitarium.Full);        //signed, and passed along from device to device

//On everyone who wants to see it
Cogitarium.AcceptSource(FingerprintFromTheSource);      //type it, scan it, read it out
Cogitarium.SegmentReceived += (Stream, Segment) => Render(Stream.Name, Segment.Level, Segment.Data);

A device that doesn't accept the source doesn't keep the stream, doesn't show it and doesn't pass it on. It is only offered to you (its name and who made it, checked against its signature), so you can look at what is around and choose:

Cogitarium.StreamOffered += Offer => Console.WriteLine($"{Offer.Name} from {Offer.Fingerprint}");
Cogitarium.Accept(Cogitarium.Offers[0]);      //only now it's kept, shown and passed on

Offers are never passed on, and only the newest 256 are kept.

Example: the further away, the smaller

Cogitarium.Want("district-12", Cogitarium.Quarter, Cogitarium.Sixteenth);   //far away: small is enough
Cogitarium.Want("district-12");                                              //nothing listed means every size

Devices only send you the sizes you want, and a device that wants only small ones is never sent the big ones.

Who gets the pieces first: the rank

Every device tells its neighbors how it is doing, and the source sends to the best first. The app feeds the numbers in (nothing here measures them):

Cogitarium.MyStatus = new Cogitarium.DeviceStatus
{
    WifiStrength = 80,         //0 to 100, higher is better
    WifiUsage = 25,            //0 to 100, how busy the wifi has been on average, lower is better
    BatteryPercent = 70,       //0 to 100
    BatteryCapacity = 4000,    //mAh, how big the battery is
};

A neighbor's rank is 0 to 100, higher gets pieces first:

rank = (30 x wifi + 15 x (100 - usage) + 25 x battery + 10 x size) / 80, where size is the battery size as a percent of FullCapacity (5000 mAh), minus 30 for every different neighbor that distrusts it. A device that said nothing counts as 50. How fast a device answers is only used to break a tie.

The source goes down its list from the best to the worst: it sends to the best InitialReceivers, and a device that refuses or doesn't answer is replaced by the next one down until enough took the piece. Every other device does the same with Fanout for the pieces it passes on. Cogitarium.Neighbors(ChunkID) lists them in that order with each one's Status, Distrusts and Rank.

Things to know:

  • The numbers are what the device says. A liar can claim a perfect score and get the pieces first. It still can't change them (they are signed), but it can drop them, and that isn't noticed. The distrust count is not said by the device: each device counts it itself.
  • Reports are checked for range (0 to 100, battery up to 1,000,000 mAh) and anything else is refused. A distrusted device's reports are ignored.
  • A source only has the status of the devices connected to it. In a big mesh each device ranks its own neighbors.
  • Set MyStatus every few seconds at most, not on every change.

Liars: ratings

The rank above is only what a device says. So the devices also rate the main devices, and the source checks them.

  • Mains. The devices the source sends to first (stream.Mains). The source stamps every copy of a piece with the main it sent that copy to, and the stamp travels with it.
  • Rating. Every RateEvery (5 s) each device rates the main that most of its pieces came through: 0 to 100, how many of the pieces that should have come really did. It is sent straight to the source, so a device has to be connected to the source to rate. A main never rates itself.
  • Receipt. A rating carries the number of the newest piece the device holds and a short hash of it. The source remembers what it sent, so it can check:
    • a hash that doesn't match, or a piece the source never sent, is a lie: the device is left out;
    • an old receipt is not a lie: the device is just behind, and it counts as red whatever number it said;
    • so nobody can rate well without really holding the latest pieces.
  • Average and liars. The source averages each main's ratings. If a small group (MinGroup, 3) rates a main very high (HighAt, 90) while at least twice as many rate it very low (LowAt, 30), or the other way round, the group is left out of the average (stream.Suspects(), Cogitarium.Suspected). Nobody is banned: it is decided again every round, so a device that stops lying is counted again.
  • Bouncing. The share of devices that are green (GreenAt, 70) is the stream.Coverage. Under TargetGreen (0.9) the source takes its worst main out and tries the best connection it hasn't tried (or tried the longest ago). If that made it worse, it goes back to the best set it has seen. It repeats until as many devices as possible are green (Cogitarium.MainsChanged).
var stream = Cogitarium.StartStream("district-12", "Cafe scene");
Cogitarium.MainsChanged += (Stream, Mains) => Console.WriteLine("now sending to " + string.Join(", ", Mains));
Cogitarium.Suspected += (Stream, Devices) => Console.WriteLine("left out: " + string.Join(", ", Devices));

foreach (var Main in stream.Ratings()) Console.WriteLine($"{Main.Main}: {Main.Mean:0} from {Main.Raters} devices, {Main.Green} green, {Main.Ignored} left out");
Console.WriteLine($"{stream.Coverage:P0} of the devices are green");

Things to know:

  • Free identities still beat it. More fake devices than real ones in the same group will outvote them, because the majority is taken as the truth.
  • A device that received nothing from a main can't blame it. It only rates a main it has pieces from. If everything stopped, Route.Auto is what helps.
  • Devices that aren't connected to the source can't rate. In a big mesh you want every device connected to the source for control (small messages), with the pieces still passed along the mesh.
  • The ratings of a changed set are forgotten, so the new set is rated fresh.

The internet or the Cogitarium

A device chooses where its stream comes from:

await Cogitarium.SetRoute("district-12", Cogitarium.Route.Internet);   //straight from the source
await Cogitarium.SetRoute("district-12", Cogitarium.Route.Mesh);       //from the other devices (the default)
await Cogitarium.SetRoute("district-12", Cogitarium.Route.Auto);       //mesh, until nothing has come for AutoAfter (6 s), then internet for AutoRetry (30 s), then mesh again
  • Internet. The source sends every piece to this device itself (it must be connected to it; at most MaxDirect (100) devices). The device tells its neighbors it wants no pieces from them, and stops passing pieces on. It comes back with false and changes nothing if no source of a stream in that chunk can be reached (or the source is full), so a device is never left with nothing.
  • Mesh. The normal way. The source stops sending to this device itself.
  • Auto. Starts as the mesh and switches when pieces stop coming.
  • A device that is on the internet route doesn't rate (there is no main to rate).

What it does about a device that changes a piece

Every piece is signed by the source, so a device that passes on a changed piece fails the signature at the next device. That device:

  1. throws the piece away and distrusts the one that handed it over (no more pieces from it, and it's never used to pass anything on),
  2. tells the other devices in the chunk.

When enough different neighbours (VotesToDistrust, default 3) say the same about a device, a device distrusts it too, even though it never saw it do anything. The source never picks a distrusted device as one of its first receivers, so the next best one takes its place.

Cogitarium.DeviceDistrusted += (Device, Reason) => Console.WriteLine($"{Device}: {Reason}");
Cogitarium.Forgive("tablet");          //if you decide it was a mistake

A repeat, a piece for a stream nobody knows, or one that's too big is not tampering: it's refused or ignored, and nobody is blamed.

Things to know

  • By default it only works between devices that are already connected (your paired group). A normal Yuuko group (group key, pinned devices, mutual TLS) refuses devices it doesn't know. For strangers use Nearby (next section).
  • A device that only drops pieces isn't noticed. Only changing them is. Votes only count from neighbours directly connected to you, but a person with many devices can still vote many times, so don't treat the number as proof.
  • Nothing is sent again to a device that missed a piece. It's for live streams, not files (use 6 - Folder Sync for those).
  • It carries bytes you give it: it doesn't make video or Gaussian splats, it only moves them. Each piece is at most 512 KB.
  • Pairing builds the node again: after Identity.NodeRestarted, call Cogitarium.Start() and join your chunks again. (A running Nearby is not affected by that.)

Strangers: Nearby

Cogitarium.Start() only works with the devices you paired. For devices you have never met (the streetpass part), call Nearby.Start() instead. It opens a second node next to your private one, with LAN discovery on and its own random identity, and puts Cogitarium on it. Your private group is not touched and still refuses strangers.

await Nearby.Start();                                  //finds devices on the same network by itself
await Nearby.Start("nearby.identity");                  //a source keeps the same identity every run, so others can keep accepting it
await Nearby.Start(null, "tcp/192.168.49.1:7447");      //also call this address right away (e.g. a Wi-Fi Direct group owner)
await Nearby.Start(MyTransport);                        //a link the app built itself (Wi-Fi Aware, a Bluetooth bridge...) as an IYuukoApi

Cogitarium.JoinChunk("district-12");                    //everything from here is the normal Cogitarium calls
Cogitarium.AcceptSource(sourceFingerprint);
Nearby.Stop();                                          //when you leave
Call What it does
Nearby.Start(IdentityPath, ConnectTo...) Opens the nearby node (Zenoh) and puts Cogitarium on it. Once
Nearby.Start(Transport, IdentityPath) The same on a link you give it. It stays yours to close
Nearby.Stop() Closes it
Nearby.IsOn / MyID / Neighbors Is it on / the random ID others see / who is connected now
Nearby.MyCard What you hand to everyone you connect to (up to 1 KB, anything: a name, a few lines of JSON)
Nearby.People / Passed / Forget(fingerprint) Everyone you met, newest first / event: you met someone (or met them again) / forget one
Nearby.MaxNeighbors Most devices it stays connected to (default 64, the newest extra one is disconnected)
Nearby.BeaconEvery How often it announces itself (default 5 s)

How it finds them: every few seconds each device announces its ID on the network, and the one with the lower ID calls the other (so only one of the two dials). The handshake is the normal Yuuko one, only without the group key, device list and certificate, because a stranger has none.

What a stranger can do on that node: connect, say which chunks it's in, hand over a card, and send stream commands. Every other command is denied, and nothing of your private group is on it. It still can't make you show a stream: you only keep streams from sources you accepted by fingerprint, and every piece is checked against the source's signature.

Streetpass: cards

Every device you connect to gets your card and gives you theirs. It is just bytes, so put in whatever the app needs.

Nearby.MyCard = Encoding.UTF8.GetBytes("Herman, likes Gaussian splats");
Nearby.Passed += Met => Console.WriteLine($"{Encoding.UTF8.GetString(Met.Card)} (met {Met.Times} times)");
foreach (var Person in Nearby.People) Console.WriteLine(Person.Fingerprint);
  • The card is whatever they said: nobody checks it. The fingerprint is the part that is checked (it comes from the handshake), so use that to recognise someone you met before.
  • Meeting the same device again within 10 minutes updates its card but isn't another time; later it is (Times goes up and Passed fires again).
  • The list is kept in memory (newest 1000). Save it yourself from Passed if you want it to survive a restart.
  • A new MyCard goes to the devices you connect to from then on.

Wi-Fi Direct and other links

Nearby assumes the devices can already reach each other: the same Wi-Fi, or a Wi-Fi Direct group that the OS has formed (to Zenoh a Wi-Fi Direct group is a network like any other, and devices in it find each other by multicast). If the group owner has a known address, pass it to Start so they call it right away. For a link Zenoh can't use (Wi-Fi Aware, Bluetooth), build it yourself as an IYuukoApi and give it to Nearby.Start(Transport): everything above it (the handshake, the mesh, the cards) is the same.

Things to know about Nearby:

  • Forming the Wi-Fi Direct group, or the Bluetooth link, is the app's job (it needs the phone's APIs). Nearby takes it from there. Only the same-network and the given-transport paths are tested.
  • The link under the handshake is not TLS (multicast discovery needs that off), so someone on the network can see who talks to whom, not what.
  • A stranger can make as many identities as it likes, so distrusting one just makes it come back with a new ID, and votes are cheap to fake.
  • It announces a random ID, not your device name and not the one from Identity.CreateNode.
  • You can't run Cogitarium on the private node and on Nearby at the same time: it runs on one.
  • Tested between devices in one process and between two real Zenoh sessions on one machine. Not tested on two physical devices, on a phone, over Wi-Fi Direct, or with many devices.
